### Compound interest

Interest that applies both to the initial principal and to the interest already accumulated. It is the "snowball" effect that makes investments grow over time — and debts too.

### Amortization

The portion of each installment that actually reduces the outstanding balance. In a loan, each payment is split between interest and amortization.

### SAC (Constant Amortization System)

A method where the principal portion is constant and interest falls each period, so the payment decreases over time. Common in Brazilian mortgages.

### Price table

A method where the payment is constant. Early on, most of the payment is interest; over time, the principal portion grows.

### Net worth

Everything you own (accounts, investments, assets) minus everything you owe (loans, mortgages, credit cards).

### Budget

A planned spending limit for a period and a scope (category, account, or label). Tracking compares actual spending with the limit.

### Diversification

Spreading investments across different assets to reduce the overall risk of the portfolio.
